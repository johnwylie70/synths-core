# Changes

## Unreleased

- The repository, with an empty engine that builds.
- The engine: 64 voices shared between 64 parts, each part with its own patch, volume, pan, pitch bend and effect sends.
- Each voice has two oscillators (sine, triangle, saw, pulse or a sample), a sub oscillator and noise (white, pink and two kinds of chip noise), with hard sync, ring modulation and unison. The saw, pulse and triangle are band-limited.
- Samples: mono or stereo, any pitch, a base note, forward and ping-pong loops, a start offset, cubic interpolation.
- A filter per voice (low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, notch; 12 or 24 dB an octave) with resonance, key tracking, an envelope and velocity.
- Three envelopes per voice (amplitude, filter, modulation) with a curve, and two LFOs (sine, triangle, saw, square, random) with delay and fade-in, that move pitch, pulse width, cutoff, loudness and pan.
- Poly, mono and legato modes, and glide.
- Distortion and a bit crusher per part; an echo, a reverb and a chorus that every part can send to; a limiter on the output.
- Events with a time, carried out on the exact sample: note on and off, all notes off, set a parameter (with a glide), pitch bend, and pitch, volume, pan, sample position and cut for a live voice by handle.
- A parameter table, defined once and handed out as JSON.
- A C interface, the same for WebAssembly and for a native library, described in docs/engine-api.md.
- A script that builds the WebAssembly module and checks it in node.
- Tests, a demo that renders phrases to WAV files, and a benchmark.
- Step macros: each part can have a table of up to 64 steps that set the wave (including noise), a pitch offset, the pulse width, a filter offset and the volume, with a loop, a release point and an option to slide between steps. They run at 50 steps a second, or any rate, or on `macro_tick` events from a tracker.
- The filter's outputs can be added together (low + band, band + high, all three), as a sound chip's filter is switched.
- The pulse width goes all the way from 0 to 1, and the pulse wave has no steady offset, so it fades to silence at the ends.
- Taps for a display, readable while sound is being made: the last 4096 frames of the output and 1024 of each part, peak and RMS levels, and the notes held and sounding on each part.
- A per-note arpeggio: the `arp` event gives one note (by handle, or the next note of a part) up to eight semitone offsets to step through, at a rate or on tracker ticks, up, down, up and down, as given or at random. Steps land on the exact frame, and a sounding note's arpeggio can be changed without starting the note again.
- Tests that everything that rings on (echo, reverb, chorus, filter, a long release) falls to exactly nothing and never puts out a denormal number.
- Samples play backwards (`osc1.reverse`, `osc2.reverse`): one-shots from their last frame, forward loops the other way round.
- A sample played faster than it was recorded is smoothed so that it does not alias harshly (`sample.antialias`, on to begin with; off gives the raw sound of an old tracker).
- The 24 dB filter resonates in both of its stages: its peak is as high as the 12 dB filter's at the same setting (it used to be about 3 dB lower, with only the first stage ringing).
- Mono and legato mode remember the notes held down: letting go of the sounding note goes back to the latest one still held.
- A hook for a sequencer inside the engine (`Sequencer`, `Engine::set_sequencer`): it is called from within the render on the exact frames it asks for, and plays voices directly: start a sample at a position and rate on a part and handle, set rate, pitch, volume, pan and position, cut, fade, step macros.
- A voice that has finished is let go at the end of its control period, so a one-shot sample's last moments no longer depend on the size of the blocks rendered.
- A sequencer can also hold a sample still (a rate of 0), queue the sample a voice carries on with at the end of its loop (ProTracker's sample swap), and give a voice an arpeggio in one call.
- A live input: `synths_render_io` and `synths_render_io_into` render with the frames a microphone has just delivered, mono or stereo. The input plays through a part (`input.part`, 31 to begin with) as if it were that part's one voice, so the part's filter, pan, effects, volume and sends shape it and its scope and meter show it. `input.gain`, `input.monitor` (off: recorded but not heard) and a noise gate (`input.gate`, `input.gate_release`). Silence coming in costs almost nothing, and the input starts on its exact frame whatever the block size.
- Voice effects on every part, before its distortion, in a fixed order: a pitch shifter (`fx.pitch`, -12 to 12 semitones, which lines each of its splices up with the wave so that it does not warble; at most 18.4 ms late), a ring modulator (`fx.ring`, `fx.ring_freq`, and `fx.ring_wave` noise for a whisper) and a comb that can be swept into a flanger (`fx.comb`, `fx.comb_freq`, `fx.comb_feedback`, `fx.comb_rate`, `fx.comb_depth`). Eight parts can use the shifter or the comb at once.
- Recording inside the engine: `synths_record_buffer` makes a sample slot to record into, and a `record` event (type 16) starts and stops on the exact frame, recording the live input, any part, or the whole output. The recording is an ordinary sample. `synths_record_read` says how far it has got, and `synths_sample_frames` and `synths_sample_read` get any sample's frames back out.
- Seven voice changers as data (robot, chipmunk, monster, cave, telephone, alien, whisper), in `synths_core::presets` and in the parameter table's JSON as `voice_presets`. `cargo run --release --example voice` renders a made-up voice through each to `target/demo`.
- A test that counts calls to the allocator while everything renders (there are none), and the benchmark times the live input and each voice effect.
- A change of a part's volume, or the master's, slides while the live input is sounding, as it does while a voice is.
- A song with repeat off no longer leaves notes droning when it ends: a sample that loops is faded out over a quarter of a second and a synth patch's note is let go, to finish as its release says. Unloading a song cuts the notes of its synth patches as well as its own parts'.
- A song's volume (the `song` event's volume command) reaches instruments that are synth patches too, through the volume of their notes, and still changes nothing on parts 0 to 31.
- A song can be one JSON file: a sample's sound can be in it as a WAV file in base64 (`"data"`), read by a small base64 reader of our own, and `native::save_in_one` writes a song that way. A WAV file with more than one channel is mixed down to one (it used to be the first channel alone).
- An instrument can carry the host's own description of its synth sound (`"synth"`): the engine keeps it through loading, saving and packing without reading it, and `synths_song_synth` hands it back. Keys a reader does not know are passed over wherever they are, and a test says so.
- For an editor playing a song through the engine: `song` commands to mute and solo channels (7, 8) and to play one pattern round and round (9), and `synths_song_replace`, which puts an edited song in the place of the playing one without a break when the two have the same shape.
- A new reverb, with types, in place of the old one, which rang like metal: `reverb.type` chooses `room` (the default), `hall`, `plate`, `cathedral` or `spring`. The first four are a feedback delay network: eight delay lines mixed by a Hadamard matrix, with a low cut, a pre-delay, early reflections and four all-pass filters a side on the way in, treble and bass that die away at their own rates, and lengths that drift a little so that no pitch rings on. The spring is a real spring's chirp, for when a boing is what is wanted.
- `reverb.decay` says in seconds how long the tail lasts (0, the default, leaves it to the type and `reverb.size`, which now makes the tail three times shorter or longer); `reverb.predelay` adds a gap before the reverb; `reverb.low_cut` keeps bass out of it. `reverb.size` and `reverb.damping` keep their numbers and their meaning; the default size is now 0.5.
- The reverb empties itself and stops working when its tail is over, so an unused reverb costs nothing; changes of type, size, decay and pre-delay while it rings do not click.
- The voice changers `monster` and `cave` are retuned for it: the monster is in a room, the cave in a dark cathedral.
- Tests that measure the reverb (how long the tail lasts against what was asked, how soon the echoes thicken, how flat the late tail's spectrum is, against the old reverb's), `cargo run --release --example reverb` renders a click, a stab and a chord through each type and through the old reverb, and the benchmark and the WebAssembly check time each type.
- Sample maps: one instrument from several recordings. A part can have a list of zones (`synths_zones_set`, 48 bytes a zone, up to 512 for a part and 2048 in all), each a sample slot for a range of keys and of velocities with the key it was recorded at, its own tuning, volume, pan, release, loop and start. A new note on a part whose oscillator 1 plays a sample picks its zones on the note's exact frame: every zone that holds the note plays (layers, four at most), a note outside every zone plays as the nearest key inside one would (or is silent, for a drum kit), zones in a round-robin group take turns key by key, and fades at a zone's edges crossfade it with a neighbour that overlaps it, by keys or by velocity, at equal power or in straight lines. A zone can play as recorded whatever the key (drums) and to its end whatever the key does (one-shots). A voice keeps its zone through bends, glides, arpeggios and `voice_pitch`; mono mode starts each note in its own zones and legato keeps the voice. `synths_zones_clear`, `synths_zones_count`, `synths_zones_flags` and `synths_zones_read`; the limits are in the parameter table's JSON. A note with one zone costs exactly what a note of a single sample costs.
- `cargo run --release --example zones` renders a made-up piano of five recordings as one note stretched and as a sample map, and a drum kit, to `target/demo/zones-*.wav`; the benchmark times a looped sample on one slot, on a map and on a map with two zones a note, and the WebAssembly check plays a map.
- Chords and arpeggios are told apart. A chord is several notes sounding together; an arpeggio is those notes one at a time, and it has two styles: one voice whose pitch jumps (what the `arp` event has always made, and still makes exactly), or notes struck afresh at each step.
- Arpeggiators in the engine play the struck ones, and everything else that is new: `arp_style` (event 17) gives the gate (how much of its step each note is held), 1 to 4 octaves, swing and a pattern of accents and rests; `arp_beat` (18) keeps an arpeggio in time with the new `tempo` parameter (id 224), so many steps to a beat, and a change of tempo is taken up at the next step; `arp_notes` (19) changes the chord, order and rate at the next step without losing the place, so a held arpeggio follows a change of chord; `arp_step` (20) steps an arpeggio that has no clock. Two more orders: 5, up and down with the top and bottom twice, and 6, all the notes together. Steps land on exact frames whatever the block size, nothing allocates, and one arpeggio never has more than 8 voices.
- A chord: `arp` with no clock and order 6, then a `note_on`, starts every note of the chord on one frame under one handle, which then moves, turns down and lets go of them all (each keeping its place in the chord). A sequencer has `Voices::chord` for it.
- The notes a display reads (`synths_notes_read`) show the note an arpeggio is on at that moment, and every note of a chord.
- The song format is version 2: an event's `chord` is its notes together, and its `arp` is an arpeggio, `{"chord": ..., "rate": "1/16", "order": ..., "octaves": ..., "gate": ..., "style": ..., "swing": ..., "accent": ...}`, with its rate as a note length (`"1/4"` to `"1/64"`, triplets `"1/8t"`, dotted `"1/8."`, counting a row as a sixteenth), in steps a row (`perRow`), a tick (`perTick`) or a second (`hz`). A version 1 song's `chord`, and in any song a `chord` with `chordSpeed`, `chordHz` or `chordOrder` beside it, is still the fast one-voice arpeggio it was, and plays to the bit as it did; it is written back as an `arp`. A song with no chord in it is still written as version 1. The packed form has a new 32-byte record for chords and the new arpeggios (and is then version 2); a song that needs none of it packs to the same bytes as before.
- The song player plays a chord on a channel as one thing, and steps struck arpeggios itself from the song's own ticks, so they keep to the rows through changes of tempo and speed, pattern loops, pausing and a song replaced as it plays.
- `cargo run --release --example arps` renders the fast arpeggio, a trance arpeggio, a pop arpeggio, block chords and `examples/songs/chords-and-arps.json` to `target/demo`.
- Arpeggio patterns: an arpeggio can follow a list of up to 32 steps in place of an order, each a note of the chord (counted up from its lowest, on into the octaves above), a rest or a tie, with an octave, an accent, a gate of its own and a slide into it. A host sets one as bytes (`synths_arp_pattern_set`) or as text (`synths_arp_pattern_text`: `"1 - 3 2 _ 1 3 5^"`, or a built-in pattern's name) in one of 64 places, and the `arp_pattern` event (21) gives it to a note. In a song it is the arp's `"pattern"`.
- The trance gate: every part has a gate after its voices and effects that chops its sound in a pattern of up to 32 levels in time with the tempo (`synths_gate_set`, `synths_gate_text`: `"x.x.xx.x"`), with `gate.depth`, `gate.rate`, `gate.smooth` (it cannot click), `gate.swing`, `gate.sends` (the echo and reverb ring through the gaps, or not) and `gate.stereo` (parameters 90 to 95). In a song it is an instrument's `"gate"`, and an event's.
- The engine counts the beat at the `tempo` parameter; the `clock` event (22) says which beat it is. While a song plays the beat is the song's, so gates and arpeggios counted in beats keep to its rows; the parameter is left as the host set it.
- `voice_vibrato` (event 23) gives sounding voices a vibrato of their own, after a delay.
- Glides, bends and vibrato as plain keys of a song's events: `"glide"` (true or a speed is the trackers' `3xx`; a time such as `"1/16"` slides the sounding note to the new one over that time), `"bend"` (`{"to": 2, "over": "1/8"}`, or `"scoop"`, `"fall"`, `"dip"`), `"slide"` and `"vibrato"`, on samples and synth patches alike, timed by the song's own tempo. The packed form carries them, the gates and the arp patterns in three lists after the samples.
- `cargo run --release --example arps` also renders `arp-pattern.wav`, `trance-gate.wav`, `arp-pattern-and-gate.wav` and `bends.wav` (from `examples/songs/bends.json`).
- A song has a mix. `"mixer"` holds the song's volume and, for each channel, a volume and sends to the reverb and the echo (its pan is the `pan` list the format always had); `"effects"` sets the reverb and the echo by the libraries' names (`reverbType`, `reverbDecay`, `reverbSize`, `reverbLevel`, `echoTime` in seconds or as a note length that follows the tempo, `echoFeedback`, `echoLevel` and the rest); `"loop"` gives the song a start and an end to go round. The packed form carries them in a last list, `MIXR`. A song with none of them plays to the bit as before.
- While a song plays, `song` commands 10 to 13 move a channel's volume, pan and two sends (slid over 20 milliseconds, so a fader does not click), and 14 and 15 set the loop's start and end. A song replaced as it plays takes the new song's faders where the edit moved them.
- `reverb.level` and `delay.level` (parameters 225 and 226): how loud the reverb and the echoes come back into the mix.
- A song channel's level meter (`synths_levels_read` with part 32 + channel) shows a note of a synth patch as well as a sample; it used to read 0 for a synth patch, whose sound is on another part.
- `cargo run --release --example arps` also renders `song-mix.wav` from `examples/songs/mix.json`.
